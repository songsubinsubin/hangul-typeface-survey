Purpose: This study examines how graphic designers perceive the tone and personality of Hangul (Korean) typefaces — and whether those impressions connect to familiar English typefaces.

What's involved: You will view 10 Hangul typefaces and rate each on visual impression scales. This takes approximately 15 minutes.

Voluntary: Participation is completely voluntary. You may stop at any time without consequence.

Privacy: All responses are anonymous. No identifying information is collected. Data will be stored securely for a minimum of 5 years, as per OCAD University research policy.

Dissemination: Findings may be published in academic papers or presented on a public research website. All data will be reported in aggregate form only — no individual responses will be identifiable.

Ethics: This study is pending review by the OCAD University Research Ethics Board. Questions about your rights as a participant may be directed to the REB at ethics@ocadu.ca.

Research Survey · Typeface Tone Perception

How do you read a typeface
you can't read?

This survey explores whether graphic designers who don't speak Korean can still perceive the tone and personality of Hangul typefaces — and whether those perceptions connect to familiar English typefaces.

You will view 10 Hangul typefaces displayed as book cover text. You do not need to read or understand Korean. We're interested in your visual and emotional impressions only.

~15 minutes 10 typefaces Anonymous
No Korean knowledge required. There are no right or wrong answers — your instinctive visual reaction is exactly what we're studying.

Before We Start

What you'll be doing

For each of the 10 typefaces, you'll complete three short tasks.

1
Rate the typeface on 10 personality scales
e.g. Formal ←———→ Casual. Choose the number that best matches your impression.
2
Select up to 3 emotion words
Pick the words that best describe the feeling this typeface gives you.
3
Match it to an English typeface
Which English typeface feels tonally most similar? Briefly explain why.
You are not being asked to read or translate the Korean text. Focus entirely on the visual impression — stroke style, weight, shape, and overall feel.
