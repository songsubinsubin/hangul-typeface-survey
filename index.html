Research Ethics · Informed Consent

Before we begin —
your consent matters

Study Title: How do native Korean speakers and non-Korean-speaking designers differ in their ability to perceive and distinguish the tonal voice of Hangul typefaces?

Student Investigator: Subin Song, Faculty of Design, OCAD University
Contact: subinsong@ocadu.ca · 825-288-3502

Faculty Supervisor: Nancy Snow, Assistant Professor, Faculty of Design, OCAD University
nsnow@faculty.ocadu.ca

Purpose: This research explores how non-Korean-speaking graphic designers perceive the tone of voice of Hangul typefaces based on their cultural experiences, and how they identify similarities between Latin and Korean typefaces.

What's involved: You will be asked to look at 10 different Hangul typefaces and rate each typeface on 10 personality scales using contrasting word pairs (e.g. Formal–Casual), then select the English typeface whose tone or personality most closely matches each Hangul typeface. Participation will take approximately 30 minutes.

Demographic data collected: Your primary working language, nationality, profession or major, linguistic fluency in Korean, and prior familiarity with Korean typefaces.

Voluntary: Participation is completely voluntary. You may decline to answer any question, or withdraw at any time prior to submission simply by closing the browser window — no data is recorded until you click Submit.

Privacy: All online survey responses are fully anonymous. No directly identifiable information (e.g. names, images) is collected, so it will not be possible to identify any specific individual from the data. Because responses are anonymous, individual data cannot be identified or removed once the survey has been submitted — please consider your participation carefully before clicking Submit.

Study period: This study runs from July 25 to August 31, 2026. Data analysis will begin after August 31, 2026.

Dissemination: Findings may be disseminated through academic publication or a design project. In both cases, only aggregated and de-identified data will be used. A summary of results will be available to participants upon request after October 2026 by contacting subinsong@ocadu.ca.

Research Survey · Typeface Tone Perception

How do you read a typeface
you can't read?

This survey explores whether graphic designers who don't speak Korean can still perceive the tone and personality of Hangul typefaces — and whether those perceptions connect to familiar English typefaces.

You will view 10 Hangul typefaces displayed with consonants, vowels, and a sample sentence. You do not need to read or understand Korean. We're interested in your visual and emotional impressions only.

~15 minutes 10 typefaces Anonymous
No Korean knowledge required. There are no right or wrong answers — your instinctive visual reaction is exactly what we're studying.

Before We Start

What you'll be doing

For each of the 10 typefaces, you'll complete three short tasks.

1
Rate the typeface on 10 personality scales
e.g. Formal ←———→ Casual. Choose the number that best matches your impression.
2
Select up to 3 emotion words
Pick the words that best describe the feeling this typeface gives you.
3
Match it to an English typeface
Which English typeface feels tonally most similar? Briefly explain why.
You are not being asked to read or translate the Korean text. Focus entirely on the visual impression — stroke style, weight, shape, and overall feel.
